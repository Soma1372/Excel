Suggested order for tonight

First: spend a short block on Fundamentals. If key signatures, chord spelling, inversions, and Roman numerals are slow, everything else becomes harder.

Second: spend the largest block on Figured Bass and Harmonic Analysis. These skills overlap heavily.

Third: practice Melody Harmonization. Focus first on choosing sensible harmonic functions and cadences, then on elegant voice leading.

Fourth: finish with Form Analysis. It is easier to refresh quickly than four-part writing.

Last: take the Mock Exam once without hints.

High-yield rule: If you are weak on voice leading, prioritize avoiding parallel perfect fifths/octaves, resolving leading tones, resolving chordal sevenths downward, keeping voices in range, and writing a convincing cadence.

What the exam explicitly excludes

So tonight's trainer stays primarily inside common-practice tonal theory.

Essential checklist before you walk into the exam

You should be able to do the following without much hesitation:

• spell major, minor, diminished, and augmented triads
• spell dominant, major, minor, half-diminished, and diminished sevenths
• identify root position, 6, 6/4, 7, 6/5, 4/3, and 4/2
• identify diatonic Roman numerals in major and minor
• recognize tonic, predominant, and dominant functions
• recognize authentic, half, deceptive, and plagal cadences
• detect parallel fifths and octaves
• resolve scale degree 7 correctly in dominant-tonic motion
• resolve chordal sevenths downward
• identify phrases, periods, binary, rounded binary, ternary, rondo, and sonata form.

Figured Bass → SATB

The bass note is supplied. Enter soprano, alto, and tenor notes using scientific pitch notation:

C4, F#4, Bb3, G3

The app checks chord membership, voice ranges, voice crossing, spacing, and obvious parallel fifths/octaves. Real four-part writing can allow multiple correct realizations, so the feedback is diagnostic rather than claiming there is only one solution.

Remember: 5/3 = root position
6 = first inversion
6/4 = second inversion
7 = root-position seventh chord
6/5 = first inversion seventh chord
4/3 = second inversion seventh chord
4/2 = third inversion seventh chord

Given Melody → Harmonic Plan

The real exam asks for four-part harmonization. Tonight, first make sure you can create a sensible harmonic skeleton.

For each soprano note, choose a Roman numeral. Then compare your plan with one strong model solution.

There can be more than one musically correct harmonization. The app grades against a model progression, but alternative solutions may also be defensible if the melody belongs to the chosen chords and the voice leading works.

Harmonic Analysis

For each chord, identify the Roman numeral and inversion. Then identify the cadence at the end of the phrase.

The examples use chorale-style SATB note collections rather than engraved notation so you can focus on harmonic recognition tonight.
