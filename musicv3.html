Section III-A — Reading Comprehension

Answer according to the passage.

Historical Repertory and Musical Progress

During much of the eighteenth century, courts, churches, opera houses, and public concert organizations regularly required new music. A successful composition could fulfill an immediate social or ceremonial purpose without necessarily entering a permanent repertory.

During the nineteenth century, however, older music assumed an increasingly important role in concert life. Conservatories trained professional performers, concert institutions expanded, and critics increasingly described certain compositions as lasting artistic monuments.

This change created a challenge for living composers. A symphonist writing after Beethoven confronted not only the technical problem of creating a new symphony but also the cultural authority of Beethoven's symphonies.

Different composers responded in different ways. Some treated historical styles and genres as sources of continuity. Others emphasized innovation, arguing that the history of music demonstrated the necessity of continued development.
